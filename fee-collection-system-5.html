Direct Phone Call

📅 Week 2 — Day 8 to 12  |  👥 Still unresponsive parents

STAGE 4

Face to Face Meeting

📅 Week 3 — Day 13 to 18  |  👥 Truly stubborn parents only
  • Catch during drop-off or pick-up time — they cannot avoid you
  • Request private 2-minute conversation — never embarrass publicly
  • Show child's progress report — remind them of value
  • Offer post-dated cheque — removes delay excuse
  • Politely mention seat may go to waitlisted students
Approach: "Could we speak privately for 2 minutes? [Child] has been doing great and I want to ensure they continue. Can we sort out the balance today? I have flexible options available."
✅ Expected: Remaining 3-5 parents = ₹30,000-50,000 collected
PROBLEM

Why Your Deadlines Are Not Working

❌ Vague deadlines like "pay soon"
❌ No follow-up after deadline passes
❌ No tracking of who paid who didn't
❌ Same polite message repeated always
1

Set Deadline Correctly — Day 0

Always use SPECIFIC date. Write in notebook: Parent | Amount | Deadline | Follow-up date. Set phone reminder for yourself one day before.

❌ WRONG: "Kindly pay soon" ✅ RIGHT: "Kindly pay before Friday 13th June"
2

One Day Before Deadline — Day -1

Send personal reminder to every pending parent. Keep it short and specific with exact amount. This removes the "I forgot" excuse completely.

"Dear [Name], tomorrow [DATE] is the last date for Rs.[AMOUNT] fee payment. Kindly arrange today itself. Thank you!"
3

On Deadline Day — Day 0 (Most Important!)

Check notebook at 6pm. NEVER let deadline pass silently — same day response always. Thank those who paid.

"Dear [Name], today was the last date. Request you to arrange by tomorrow morning. Please confirm when you can pay."
4

Day After Deadline — Day +1

Call every parent who still has not paid. Get NEW specific commitment date. Note their exact words and promised date in notebook.

Call: "Hello [Name], can you arrange today? Even partial payment is fine. When exactly can you pay?"
5

3 Days After Deadline — Day +3

Final message before face-to-face. Tone becomes firm but respectful. Mention continuation of classes for first time. Then meet face-to-face.

"Dear [Name], Rs.[AMOUNT] is significantly overdue. Kindly arrange immediately to ensure [Child]'s classes continue without interruption."

The 7 Golden Rules

Follow these every single time without exception

1
Every deadline must have a follow-up date set in advance. When you give a deadline, immediately set a phone reminder for Day -1, Day 0, and Day +1.
2
Never let a deadline pass silently. Same day response when deadline is missed. This is the most critical rule — one missed follow-up teaches parents that deadlines are not real.
3
Always get a new commitment date when deadline is missed. Ask "When exactly can you pay?" and note their answer. That becomes the mini-deadline.
4
Sunday review without fail — 10 minutes every week. Check tracker, mark who paid, schedule follow-ups for next week. Skipping Sunday review breaks the entire system.
5
Never threaten child's education in public. Always keep conversations private. Public embarrassment creates enemies — private conversations create solutions.
6
Acknowledge every payment with a thank you message. This builds goodwill, encourages referrals, and makes the parent feel valued — not just like a debtor.
7
Offer installments before escalating. Remove every excuse before going firm. Most parents who "cannot pay" CAN pay in installments — give them that option first.

🎯 The Core Principle

Parents pay when they believe the deadline is real. Deadlines become real only when followed up consistently. One missed follow-up = future deadlines ignored. The system only works when all 7 rules are followed together.

🧠 Psychological Triggers That Work

Use these naturally in conversation — never aggressively

👥 Social Proof

"Most parents have already cleared their dues this month."
Creates social pressure without direct confrontation. Nobody wants to be the only one who hasn't paid.

📊 Value Reminder

Show child's latest test scores or progress report during conversation.
Reminds parents what they are getting for the money. Hard to say "I won't pay" after seeing their child's improvement.

🔄 Flexibility Offer

"We accept installments — even Rs.2,000 to start is fine."
Removes the "I cannot afford it" excuse. If they say no to installments, you know they are avoiding, not struggling.

📝 Post-dated Cheque

"You can give a cheque dated for next week if that helps."
Removes the "I have no cash today" excuse. Gets a commitment with a specific date attached.

⏳ Scarcity (Last Resort)

"We have students on the waitlist — I want to secure your child's seat first."
Creates fear of losing the spot. Use this only in Stage 4 — not earlier or it loses impact.

🎁 Reciprocity

"[Child] has shown great improvement this month — really proud of them."
Triggers natural human obligation. People feel guilty not paying after receiving good news about their child.

📅 Specific Numbers

"The balance of Rs.8,400 is pending since [EXACT DATE]."
Specific numbers feel more real and serious than round numbers. Shows you are tracking carefully.
